Details about parameters#

This section describes special parameters in the DANRA dataset.

DANRA Parameters#

DANRA output is ordered into three main categories based on the vertical levels of the data: surface or single level (SL), height levels (HL), and pressure levels (PL). Each category contains various meteorological parameters, which are described in detail below. Each category is available as an analysis dataset and two forecast datasets (see Data availability); the tables below show which parameters are available in which dataset.

Special Parameters

Special parameters

The following parameters are encoded using a different schema than WMO standards. Thus, we provide a brief description of those below.

Precipitation type

The precipitation type, prtp, describes the most prevalent type of precipitation in the given time frame. Where there is no precipitation the value is missing (NaN). The following types are available:

Code

Description

0

Drizzle

1

Rain

2

Sleet

3

Snow

4

Freezing drizzle

5

Freezing rain

6

Graupel

7

Hail

Icing index

The icing index, icei, is a measure of the risk of icing in the atmosphere. The index is calculated as the maximum risk of icing somewhere in the atmosphere column. The index is an integer between 1 and 4, corresponding to the following risk levels. Where there is no risk of icing the value is missing (NaN).

Code

Description

1

Trace

2

Light

3

Moderate

4

Severe

AROME Hail diagnostic

The hail diagnostic, xhail, is a measure of the risk of hail. It is the maximum total column graupel in kg/m^2 since previous post-processing. Values above 16 kg/m^2 are considered to be a risk of hail.

Missing values

Some parameters are only defined in part of the domain or under certain conditions. Where they are undefined, the value is missing and is read as NaN by xarray:

  • cb_column and ct_column: where there are no clouds.

  • prtp: where there is no precipitation.

  • icei: where there is no risk of icing.

  • albedo, soil_ice, soil_moisture, surface_soil_moisture, soil_temperature and t_surface_isba: outside the land surface scheme, i.e. over sea. Use the missing values, not lsm, to find where these are valid, since the land surface scheme also covers some points that lsm classifies as sea.

Surface temperatures

  • t0m is the surface (skin) temperature, averaged over all surface types in the grid box.

  • t_surface_isba is the surface (skin) temperature of the land surface only.

  • soil_temperature is the mean temperature of the root-zone soil layer.

Radiation

The instantaneous and accumulated radiation parameters hold different quantities, even where their names are similar:

Parameter

Quantity

lwavr

Net surface long-wave radiation (downwelling minus upwelling)

swavr

Net surface short-wave radiation (downwelling minus reflected)

lwavr_accum_past_hr

Downwelling surface long-wave radiation

swavr_accum_past_hr

Direct (beam) short-wave radiation on a horizontal surface

grad_accum_past_hr

Global radiation: direct plus diffuse downwelling short-wave radiation

dni_accum_past_hr

Direct normal irradiance, perpendicular to the solar beam

nlwrs_accum_past_hr

Net surface long-wave radiation

nswrs_accum_past_hr

Net surface short-wave radiation

albedo is only valid in daylight.

Accumulated and interval parameters

These are only available in the forecast datasets.

  • *_accum_past_hr parameters are accumulated over the hour ending at the valid time. Radiation and heat fluxes are therefore in W s m-2 (J m-2); divide by 3600 to get the mean flux in W m-2. Precipitation amounts are in kg m-2, which is equal to mm. Very small negative values can occur due to numerical noise.

  • ugst10m_past_hr and vgst10m_past_hr are the components of the maximum wind gust over the hour ending at the valid time.

  • tmax2m_since_analysis and tmin2m_since_analysis are the maximum and minimum 2 m temperature since the start of the forecast, so the period they cover grows with the lead time.

For why DANRA provides both 3-hourly and 18-hourly forecasts, and which to use for accumulated precipitation, see Accumulated variables, forecasting and data assimilation.

Sign conventions

The surface fluxes wevap_accum_past_hr, snsub_accum_past_hr, lhe_accum_past_hr, lhsub_accum_past_hr and sshf_accum_past_hr are positive downwards, so evaporation, sublimation and upward heat fluxes are negative. The momentum fluxes uflx_accum_past_hr and vflx_accum_past_hr are positive upwards, so they are negative under a westerly and southerly wind, respectively.

Freezing level

freezing_height is the height of the 0 °C isotherm. Where the whole atmospheric column is below freezing, the value is extrapolated below the surface and can be negative.
